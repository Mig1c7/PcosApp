What works, and how sure we are

Sorted by strength of evidence, so you can stop weighing every article against every other one.

ApproachWhat it helpsEvidence
Combined oral contraceptive pillRegular bleeds, acne, unwanted hair, protects the womb lining. First-line medicine in the guideline; no single brand is preferred, lower doses favoured.Strong
Regular movement and balanced eatingInsulin sensitivity, mood, sleep, long-term heart and diabetes risk. No specific diet has been shown to beat another, so the best pattern is one you can keep.Strong
MetforminMainly metabolic features: glucose, insulin resistance, sometimes cycles. Start low and increase slowly to limit stomach upset; B12 is worth checking on long-term use.Strong

When to contact a doctor soon

  • Hair growth, acne or scalp hair loss that appears or worsens quickly over weeks to months, a deepening voice, or increased muscle bulk.
  • No period for three months or more when you are not on hormonal contraception, or very heavy or prolonged bleeding.
  • Easy bruising, purple stretch marks, or new muscle weakness in the thighs and upper arms.
  • Low mood, anxiety or eating worries that are getting in the way of daily life. If you ever have thoughts of harming yourself, contact your local emergency number or a crisis line straight away.

    Sources

    Where the information here comes from, and what to keep in mind when reading it.

    • Teede HJ et al. Recommendations from the 2023 International Evidence-based Guideline for the Assessment and Management of PCOS. The main basis for diagnosis, screening and treatment statements.
    • Monash University / MCHRI: guideline algorithms. Diagnostic pathway, cycle definitions, androgen testing.
    • Medical Journal of Australia: summary of the 2023 guideline.
    • Goodarzi MO, Carmina E, Azziz R. DHEA, DHEAS and PCOS (2015). Adrenal androgen excess in roughly 20–30% of people with PCOS; cause still unclear.
    • Non-classic congenital adrenal hyperplasia: screening with 17-hydroxyprogesterone. Basis for the 200 and 1000 ng/dL (6 and 30 nmol/L) thresholds.
    • Mayo Clinic Laboratories test catalog. General reference intervals; your own lab's printed range takes priority.
    • PCOS and mental health: systematic review and meta-analysis.

    About the term "adrenal PCOS". It is not a formal medical diagnosis. It is a useful shorthand for PCOS in which the adrenal androgen DHEA-S is raised. Treatment follows the same guideline as any PCOS, with extra care taken to rule out adrenal conditions that look similar.

    About reference ranges. Ranges differ between laboratories and testing methods, especially for testosterone and DHEA-S. General ranges here are approximations for women in their early twenties.
